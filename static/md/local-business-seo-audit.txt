# Illucrum Local Business SEO Audit

The Illucrum Local Business SEO Audit is a fixed-price, point-by-point review of how a local business shows up when people nearby search for what it does. It covers 54 points in 9 sections: the website's technical basics, the Google Business Profile, reviews, local pages and markup, listings on other platforms, local rankings across the service area, local links, calls and inquiries, and AI answers. It is built for businesses that serve customers in a defined area, from their own premises or at the customer's location, and it weighs the Business Profile, reviews and listings as heavily as the website.

You receive a written report with every point scored and a prioritized list of fixes. The price is fixed, published and the same for everyone. The report is the end of the transaction: no retainer, no subscription, and nothing sold afterward.

## Who this audit is for

- Trades and home services: plumbers, electricians, builders, cleaners
- Clinics, dentists, salons and other appointment-based businesses
- Restaurants, shops and other businesses customers visit in person
- Local professional firms, such as law and accounting practices, whose clients come from nearby
- Service-area businesses with no storefront, which visit their customers

**A different audit may fit better if:**

- You have an office but win most of your work well beyond your area: the Website SEO Audit (54 points)
- You sell mainly online and ship to customers: the E-commerce SEO Audit (54 points)
- Your main concern is how AI assistants describe your business, beyond local searches: the GEO Audit (51 points)

### 1. Technical Foundations (7 points)

The website's technical basics, focused on what a local business site needs.

| # | Point | What it looks at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Page Speed & Core Web Vitals | How quickly key pages load and respond, especially on phones, where most local searches happen. |
| 2 | Mobile Usability | Whether the site reads and works properly on a phone. |
| 3 | HTTPS & SSL Certificate | Whether every page is served securely, with a valid certificate. |
| 4 | Index Coverage | Whether Google has indexed the homepage, service pages and location pages, and why any are left out. |
| 5 | XML Sitemap & Robots.txt | Whether crawlers can reach and find every important page. |
| 6 | Canonicals & Duplicate Pages | Whether each page has one main address, and whether any pages duplicate each other. |
| 7 | Redirects & Broken Links | Links that lead to missing pages, and redirects that pass through several steps. |

## What you get

- **A written report** covering every point, each scored from 1 to 10 and marked Good, Needs attention or Critical, with a plain-English explanation of what was found.
- **A prioritized fix list.** Anything that stops search engines or AI crawlers from reaching or indexing the site comes first, then other critical issues, then quick improvements, then the rest.
- **Findings specific to your site.** Every finding refers to real pages, figures and examples from your site, not generic advice.
- **Dated figures.** Rankings, links, reviews and AI answers are recorded with the date they were seen, so the report also works as a baseline you can compare against yourself.
- **Points that don't apply are marked N/A** and left out of the scoring, with a short note on why.
- **A dated record of local visibility:** where the business appeared, from which points in the service area, on which date, and which businesses it competes with in the map results.
- **Yours to act on.** The report is written so you can make the fixes yourself, hand it to your developer, or hire anyone you like.

## What Illucrum needs from you

- **Read access to Google Search Console** (a restricted user is enough) **and Google Analytics 4** (viewer access).
- **Manager access to your Google Business Profile.** It is used only to read settings and performance data. Without it, the audit reads the public profile and one point is marked N/A.
- **Your correct business details:** name, address, phone number, website, opening hours, service area and main services. Every listing is checked against them.
- **Whether customers come to you, you go to them, or both.** Google treats these differently.
- **How you currently ask customers for reviews,** if you do.
- **The searches you want to win,** such as your main services with your town or area.
- **Nothing is changed** on your site or in your accounts. Access is used only to read.
- **If you'd rather not give access,** the points that depend on it are marked N/A and the report says which. Nothing is guessed.

## What's not included

- **Implementing the fixes.** The audit finds and prioritizes the issues; the work itself is yours to do or assign.
- **Ongoing monitoring, retainers or subscriptions.**
- **Forecasts of traffic, leads or revenue,** and guarantees of rankings.
- **Managing your profile or listings:** posting updates, replying to reviews, creating listings or removing duplicates.
- **Asking your customers for reviews.**
- **Anything sold afterward.** Nothing is pitched during or after the audit.

## Limits, stated up front

- **One location in depth.** For a business with several locations, the main location is audited in full and the others are spot-checked. The report says which.
- **Local rankings depend on where the searcher is.** Rankings are checked from set points across your service area, on the audit date. A different street can show different results.
- **Profile performance data needs manager access.** Without it, only the public profile can be read.
- **Reviews and links are a snapshot,** recorded as of the audit date. The audit doesn't report links gained or lost over time, because no reliable history of them is available.
- **Google doesn't publish its local ranking formula.** The audit follows Google's own guidance and Business Profile policies, plus established practitioner research, and the report says which is which.
- **Your contact form may receive one test message,** clearly marked as a test, to confirm that inquiries arrive.

## Frequently asked questions

### What counts as a local business for the Illucrum Local Business SEO Audit?

Any business that serves customers in a defined area, either at its own premises or at the customer's location: trades, clinics, salons, restaurants, shops, and local law or accounting firms. If you win most of your work well beyond your area, the Website SEO Audit fits better.

### I don't have a storefront. I visit my customers. Is this audit for me?

Yes. Service-area businesses have their own Google rules for how the address and service area are shown, and the audit checks them, along with visibility across the whole area you serve.

### My business has several locations. What does the audit cover?

The main location is audited in full, and the others are spot-checked for the most common problems. The report says which locations were covered and how.

### Will Illucrum post on my profile or contact my customers?

No. Access is used only to read. Nothing on your profile, website or listings is changed, and no one is contacted on your behalf.

### Does Illucrum fix the issues the Local Business SEO Audit finds?

No. The audit is the deliverable. The report is written so you can act on it yourself, hand it to your developer, or hire anyone you like.

### Is the price the same for everyone?

Yes. Each Illucrum audit has one fixed price, published on illucrum.com. There are no tiers and no custom quotes.

### When do I pay?

You receive an invoice when you decide to go ahead. The audit is carried out, and the report is sent once the invoice is paid.

### What happens after I receive the report?

Nothing else. There is no follow-up sale, subscription or retainer. The report is yours to use as you see fit.

### Is the report automated?

No. Every point is checked and written up by Szymon Kokot, and every finding refers to your site specifically.

### Can the Local Business SEO Audit guarantee better rankings?

No. Rankings depend on Google and on your competitors, and no one can guarantee them. The audit shows what is holding the site back and which fixes to make first; results depend on what is done with it.
