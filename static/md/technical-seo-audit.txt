# Illucrum Technical SEO Audit

The Illucrum Technical SEO Audit is a fixed-price, point-by-point review of the machinery of a website against 36 points in 9 sections: crawling and indexing, site architecture, JavaScript rendering, speed, mobile experience, security, structured data, international targeting and AI crawler access. It suits any site that already knows what to say and needs to know whether anything technical is in the way. It goes deeper on these questions than the site-type audits, and leaves out keywords, content, competitors, trust and conversion.

You receive a written report with every point scored and a prioritized list of fixes. The price is fixed, published and the same for everyone. The report is the end of the transaction: no retainer, no subscription, and nothing sold afterward.

### 1. Crawlability & Indexation (7 points)

Whether search engines can reach the pages that matter, and index those and not others.

| # | Point | What it looks at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Robots.txt Configuration | Whether crawler rules allow access to everything that should be found, and block only what shouldn't. |
| 2 | XML Sitemap Health | Whether sitemaps are valid, complete, current, and free of pages that shouldn't be listed. |
| 3 | Index Coverage & Excluded Pages | What Google has indexed, what it has excluded, and whether the reasons point to problems. |
| 4 | HTTP Status Codes & Server Errors | Whether pages return the right responses, and whether server errors or false "not found" pages occur. |
| 5 | Redirect Chains & Loops | Redirects that pass through several steps or loop back on themselves. |
| 6 | Crawl Budget & Index Bloat | Whether crawlers spend their time on the pages that matter, or on low-value addresses the site generates. |
| 7 | Orphan Pages | Pages that exist but aren't linked from anywhere on the site. |

### 2. Site Architecture & URLs (5 points)

How the site is organized: addresses, canonicals, internal links, depth and pagination.

| # | Point | What it looks at |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | URL Structure & Consistency | Whether page addresses are readable, consistent and stable. |
| 9 | Canonicalization & Duplicate URLs | Whether each piece of content has one main address, with duplicates pointing to it. |
| 10 | Internal Linking & Link Equity Flow | Whether internal links lead crawlers, and ranking strength, to the most important pages. |
| 11 | Site Depth & Click Distance | How many clicks it takes to reach important pages from the homepage. |
| 12 | Pagination Handling | Whether content spread across numbered pages can be found and crawled. |

### 3. Rendering & JavaScript (3 points)

Whether the content crawlers need is in the page they receive, and whether scripts delay what matters.

| # | Point | What it looks at |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | JavaScript Rendering & Content Parity | Whether content, links and tags are visible without running scripts, which is how many crawlers see a page. |
| 14 | Render-Blocking Resources | Files that hold up the first display of the page. |
| 15 | Lazy-Loading of Critical Content | Whether delayed loading hides important content from crawlers or slows down the main content. |

### 4. Speed & Core Web Vitals (5 points)

How fast the site loads and responds for real visitors, and what slows it down.

| # | Point | What it looks at |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | Page Speed (Mobile & Desktop) | How quickly each page template loads on phones and computers. |
| 17 | Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS) | Google's measures of real visitor experience: how fast the main content appears, how quickly the page responds, and whether the layout jumps while it loads. |
| 18 | Image Optimization & Modern Formats | Whether images are sized, compressed and served in modern formats. |
| 19 | Caching, Compression & CDN | Whether the site uses caching, compression and content delivery to serve pages quickly. |
| 20 | Third-Party Script Weight | How much tracking, chat, advertising and other third-party code slows pages down. |

### 5. Mobile & Page Experience (3 points)

Whether the site works on phones, where Google does most of its crawling and where most visitors arrive.

| # | Point | What it looks at |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | Mobile Rendering | Whether the mobile version shows the same content, links and markup as the desktop version. |
| 22 | Viewport & Tap Targets | Whether pages fit the screen, and buttons and links are easy to tap. |
| 23 | Intrusive Interstitials | Whether pop-ups or overlays block the content on phones. |

### 6. Security & HTTPS (4 points)

Whether the site is served securely and exposes nothing it shouldn't. Observed from the outside: this is not a security test.

| # | Point | What it looks at |
|---|---|---|
| 24 | HTTPS Enforcement & SSL Certificate | Whether every page is served securely, with a valid certificate, and insecure versions redirect. |
| 25 | Mixed Content | Whether secure pages load any images, scripts or files over an insecure connection. |
| 26 | Security Headers | Whether the site sends the standard security instructions browsers use to protect visitors. |
| 27 | Exposed Files & Endpoints | Whether files, test sites or admin areas that should be private are publicly reachable or indexed. |

### 7. Structured Data & Directives (3 points)

The machine-readable layer: structured data, instructions to search engines and social metadata.

| # | Point | What it looks at |
|---|---|---|
| 28 | Structured Data Validity | Whether structured markup is valid, matches the visible page, and is eligible for rich results. |
| 29 | Robots Directives & Snippet Controls | Whether page-level instructions to search engines, such as noindex and snippet limits, are set as intended, including settings that affect Google's AI features. |
| 30 | Open Graph & Social Metadata | How pages look when they are shared on social platforms and in messaging apps. |

### 8. International & Parameter Handling (3 points)

Language and region targeting, domain consistency, and addresses created by parameters and filters.

| # | Point | What it looks at |
|---|---|---|
| 31 | Hreflang Implementation | For sites in more than one language or country, whether each version is correctly linked to the others. Marked N/A for single-language sites. |
| 32 | Host & Protocol Consistency | Whether every version of the domain, with or without www and on http or https, leads to one preferred version. |
| 33 | Parameter & Faceted URL Handling | Whether addresses created by tracking codes, filters and sorting are kept from producing duplicates. |

### 9. Machine & AI Access (3 points)

Whether AI crawlers and assistants can reach and understand the site. The technical layer only: whether assistants actually cite the business is covered by the GEO Audit.

| # | Point | What it looks at |
|---|---|---|
| 34 | AI Crawler Access | Whether the crawlers behind AI assistants are allowed through, both by crawler rules and by firewalls or bot protection. |
| 35 | Machine-Readable Content | Whether the site's core facts, such as what the business does, its prices and contact details, are available in plain text that machines can read easily. |
| 36 | Entity Structured Data (Organization & sameAs) | Whether the site tells machines clearly who the business is, and links to its profiles elsewhere. |

## What you get

- **A written report** covering every point, each scored from 1 to 10 and marked Good, Needs attention or Critical, with a plain-English explanation of what was found.
- **A prioritized fix list.** Anything that stops search engines or AI crawlers from reaching or indexing the site comes first, then other critical issues, then quick improvements, then the rest.
- **Findings specific to your site.** Every finding refers to real pages, figures and examples from your site, not generic advice.
- **Dated figures.** Rankings, links, reviews and AI answers are recorded with the date they were seen, so the report also works as a baseline you can compare against yourself.
- **Points that don't apply are marked N/A** and left out of the scoring, with a short note on why.
- **Findings a developer can act on.** Each technical issue names the pages or templates affected.
- **Yours to act on.** The report is written so you can make the fixes yourself, hand it to your developer, or hire anyone you like.

## What Illucrum needs from you

- **Read access to Google Search Console** (a restricted user is enough).
- **Context on recent changes,** such as a migration, redesign or traffic drop, if there is one you want looked at.
- **Nothing is changed** on your site or in your accounts. Access is used only to read.
- **If you'd rather not give access,** the points that depend on it are marked N/A and the report says which. Nothing is guessed.

## What's not included

- **Implementing the fixes.** The audit finds and prioritizes the issues; the work itself is yours to do or assign.
- **Ongoing monitoring, retainers or subscriptions.**
- **Forecasts of traffic, leads or revenue,** and guarantees of rankings.
- **Keyword research, content review, competitor comparison, trust and conversion checks.** These are covered by the Website, SaaS, E-commerce and Local Business audits.
- **Penetration testing or a security audit.**
- **Anything sold afterward.** Nothing is pitched during or after the audit.

## Limits, stated up front

- **The technical layer only.** The audit leaves out keywords, content quality, competitors, trust signals and conversion.
- **Checked from the outside.** Server and hosting configuration is judged by what the site returns, without access to the server itself.
- **Security points are observations, not testing.** The audit notes what is publicly visible. It is not a penetration test or a security audit.
- **Very large sites are reviewed by template.** Content that only appears after a visitor interacts with the page may be missed. The report states the date and size of the crawl.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is the Illucrum Technical SEO Audit different from the Website SEO Audit?

The Technical SEO Audit covers only the technical layer, in 36 points and in more depth. The Website SEO Audit includes a shorter technical section and adds on-page SEO, keywords, backlinks, competitors, trust, user experience and AI search visibility.

### When is a Technical SEO Audit the right choice?

Before or after a migration, redesign or platform change; when traffic drops without an obvious cause; or when a developer needs a precise list of what to fix. If you also want to know what to say and who you are up against, a site-type audit fits better.

### Is the Technical SEO Audit a security audit?

No. It notes what is publicly visible, such as the certificate, security headers and exposed files. It doesn't test the site for vulnerabilities.

### Does Illucrum fix the issues the Technical SEO Audit finds?

No. The audit is the deliverable. The report is written so you can act on it yourself, hand it to your developer, or hire anyone you like.

### Is the price the same for everyone?

Yes. Each Illucrum audit has one fixed price, published on illucrum.com. There are no tiers and no custom quotes.

### When do I pay?

You receive an invoice when you decide to go ahead. The audit is carried out, and the report is sent once the invoice is paid.

### What happens after I receive the report?

Nothing else. There is no follow-up sale, subscription or retainer. The report is yours to use as you see fit.

### Is the report automated?

No. Every point is checked and written up by Szymon Kokot, and every finding refers to your site specifically.

### Can the Technical SEO Audit guarantee better rankings?

No. Rankings depend on Google and on your competitors, and no one can guarantee them. The audit shows what is holding the site back and which fixes to make first; results depend on what is done with it.
