01 / Quick answer

What is a SaaS SEO audit?

The featured-snippet answer, in plain language. The rest of the page expands every claim.

A SaaS SEO audit is a structured review of how your software website earns organic trials, demos, and pipeline. Built for B2B and B2C SaaS companies with trial, demo, or freemium models, it diagnoses 52 points across eleven sections: technical health (including JavaScript rendering and app subdomain handling), funnel-adapted on-page SEO, funnel-stage keyword strategy, trial and demo page review, feature and integration pages, programmatic SEO potential, content gap and blog strategy, competitor and SERP analysis, backlink profile, conversion UX, and AI search readiness. The output is a prioritised written report, delivered in 5 to 8 business days, telling your team what to fix first and what to skip.

04 / Scope

The eleven sections of a SaaS SEO audit

Each section is reviewed against SaaS-specific benchmarks, not generic SEO checklists. The audit framework is documented in full; below is the summary, with the point count for each section.

  1. Technical SEO, SaaS-adapted (10 points)

    Page speed, Core Web Vitals, HTTPS, mobile rendering, crawlability, canonicals, structured data, and indexation health, plus the SaaS-specific additions: JavaScript rendering and indexability, app subdomain handling, and login-wall content. Only 47% of sites currently pass Core Web Vitals; SaaS sites built on JavaScript-heavy frameworks fail most often here.

  2. On-page SEO, funnel-adapted (8 points)

    Title tags, meta descriptions, headings, content depth, internal linking architecture, plus dedicated reviews of the homepage's positioning clarity, the pricing page, and the feature pages. SaaS pages are scored by funnel stage: a homepage against brand-intent benchmarks, a pricing page against pricing-intent searchers, a feature page against capability-specific queries.

  3. Funnel keyword strategy (7 points)

    Funnel-stage keyword coverage, "best [category]" and comparison keyword visibility, ranking keyword overview, branded versus non-branded split, target keyword mapping, keyword placement, and intent alignment. Search-volume vanity metrics are deprioritised in favour of intent quality, because SaaS keywords with 200 monthly searches often outperform 20,000-volume head terms in trial conversion, and comparison queries convert at 8 to 15%.

  4. Trial and demo page audit (3 points)

    The pages that most directly turn organic traffic into revenue, and which no generic audit reviews at all. Trial, demo, and signup page SEO; the trust signals present on those conversion pages; and the clarity and hierarchy of the CTAs pointing at them.

  5. Feature and integration pages (2 points)

    Integration page SEO against partner-tool searches, and use case or persona page coverage. Most SaaS products underinvest badly here; companies with 50+ integration pages often get 20 to 30% of their organic traffic from integration searches alone.

  6. Programmatic SEO potential (1 point, Full audit only)

    Whether template-based content at scale (integrations, use cases, industries, job roles, locations) would generate meaningful organic traffic for your product, whether competitors have already scaled it, and whether your stack can actually generate the pages.

  7. Content gap and blog strategy (2 points, Full audit only)

    Blog and resource-hub presence, publishing cadence, whether content links back to product pages, and the keyword gap against competitors. SaaS sites usually need three to seven content clusters (one per primary use case), not the dozens that informational sites accumulate.

  8. Competitor and SERP analysis (5 points)

    The top three organic competitors are identified, authority scores compared, and G2, Capterra, and Product Hunt profile consistency checked. The Full audit adds competitor content benchmarking and SERP feature opportunities. It is the section most generic audits skip entirely.

  9. Backlink profile (3 points, Full audit only)

    Referring domain count and growth, anchor text distribution, and link gap analysis against competitors. The average DR across 28,250 SaaS sites is 62.6; the audit identifies whether the gap is closeable in 6 months or 18.

  10. UX and conversion, SaaS-adapted (5 points)

    Signup and trial flow friction (required form fields, credit card up front, unclear benefit statements), page layout and readability, dwell time risk, accessibility basics, and, in the Full audit, overall funnel coherence. Most SaaS sites have a conversion problem long before they have a traffic problem. The audit isolates which is biting first.

  11. AI search and answer engine optimisation (6 points)

    Whether your product is recommended when buyers ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Gemini for "the best tool for X", your presence in Google AI Overviews on category and comparison queries, AI crawler access, and documentation indexability. The Full audit adds your G2, Capterra, and Reddit citation footprint, answer-ready comparison content, and product entity structured data. Only 20% of URLs cited by AI engines also rank in Google's top 10, which is why this is reviewed as a separate discipline.

Tier split

The Basic audit covers 35 of the 52 points, across 8 of the 11 sections, and is right for SaaS sites under 100 pages where the priorities are well-defined. The Full audit covers all 11 sections (52 points), adding programmatic SEO potential, content gap and blog strategy, and the backlink profile. It is the right call when the site is established, the stakes are higher, or off-site authority is a serious bottleneck.

05 / What comes back

What typically comes back in a SaaS audit

Patterns that repeat across SaaS audits, anonymised. These are the kinds of findings the report explains in detail, with scored severity and prioritised fix order.

  • 01

    Pricing page on page 2

    A pricing page ranking on page 2 for the company's own pricing keyword, because the H1 names the product rather than the search term.

  • 02

    Integration pages with no URLs

    Integration pages that exist as anchor links in the footer but have no dedicated URL, sacrificing 15 to 25% of recoverable organic traffic.

  • 03

    Wrong page winning

    A homepage targeting a high-volume informational keyword that does not convert, while a low-traffic comparison page is converting at 11% and getting almost no internal links.

  • 04

    Missing schema on feature pages

    Schema markup missing or misconfigured on every feature page, blocking rich-result eligibility in SaaS comparison searches where star ratings, FAQ schema, and app metadata change the click decision.

  • 05

    Blog zombies and cannibalisation

    A 200-page blog with one or two posts driving 80% of the search traffic, the rest cannibalising each other or sitting in zombie territory (indexed, no traffic, no internal links).

  • 06

    Subdomain leaking authority

    A subdomain blog that should be a subdirectory, leaking authority from the marketing site.

The report is specific to the site. No generic findings. No filler.

08 / FAQ

SaaS SEO audit FAQ

The questions that come up most often before a SaaS audit. The full FAQ has more.

Why does SaaS need a different SEO approach than other businesses?

SaaS buyers move across multiple search-intent stages over weeks or months: awareness, consideration, decision. A generic audit treats every keyword equally; a SaaS audit weights bottom-of-funnel and comparison queries, because that is where trial conversions actually happen. The competitive context is also unforgiving; SaaS head terms like 'best CRM' are among the most expensive and contested searches on the internet.

What ROI can I realistically expect from SaaS SEO?

The published 3-year benchmark for B2B SaaS SEO ROI is 702%, with an average break-even of about 7 months. Long-term organic CAC for SaaS companies with strong content engines can drop to around â¬290 per customer, against a median paid-search CAC of around â¬802 per customer. The audit identifies which bets are likely to compound and which are not.

Is this an automated tool, or a real audit?

A real audit, delivered by a person. Some of the data is gathered using automated processes, but all the analysis and conclusion are done by a real person. If you are deciding between an audit and a tool subscription, see SEO audit vs SEO tool.

What about ongoing SEO work after the audit?

The audit is a self-contained deliverable. You can implement the report yourself, or scope a separate fixed-price project with us for the highest-priority fixes. We do not sell monthly retainers, and there is no obligation to continue. The decision logic is laid out in SEO audit vs SEO retainer.

Do you audit pre-launch SaaS websites?

Not usually. Pre-launch sites have too few data signals for a meaningful audit. The exception is if you are about to launch and want the technical and on-page foundations validated before paid traffic starts; that scope is closer to a pre-launch readiness check, which we can scope as a Basic audit.

How long does the audit take to deliver?

Five to eight business days, whichever tier you pick, counted from the moment we have your brief and the access we need. The window reflects the manual review; a real audit cannot be compressed below it without cutting the part that produces the findings.
