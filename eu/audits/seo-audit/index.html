01 / Quick answer

What is an SEO audit?

The featured-snippet answer, in plain language.

An SEO audit is a structured diagnosis of why a website is not ranking, converting, or growing the way it should. For small businesses and SMEs, the Illucrum audit reviews 52 points across eight sections: technical SEO, on-page SEO, keyword opportunities, competitor analysis, local SEO, UX and conversion signals, AI search readiness, and (in the Full version) the backlink profile. The output is a written report with scored severity and prioritised fixes, delivered in 3 to 8 business days.

03 / Scope

The eight sections of an SEO audit

The 52 audit points are grouped into eight sections. Every check is scored on severity (green / amber / red) and prioritised by likely impact.

  1. Technical SEO (12 points)

    Page speed and Core Web Vitals, mobile friendliness, HTTPS, XML sitemap and robots configuration, canonical handling, structured data, crawl errors and index coverage, redirect chains and 4xx errors, duplicate and thin pages, and hreflang on multilingual sites. Most quiet ranking suppressors hide here.

  2. On-page SEO (10 points)

    Title tags, meta descriptions, heading structure, image alt text, content quality and length, and internal linking, plus URL structure, social meta tags, content freshness, and keyword placement in the Full audit. This is also where cannibalisation and zombie pages (indexed, no traffic) surface.

  3. Keyword opportunities (6 points)

    Ranking keyword overview, striking-distance keywords sitting in positions 4 to 20, and local keyword coverage. The Full audit adds search intent alignment, the keyword gap against competitors, and the branded versus non-branded traffic split.

  4. Backlink profile (5 points, Full audit only)

    Total backlinks and referring domains, toxic and spammy links, anchor text distribution, links lost and gained over the last 90 days, and the link gap against direct competitors. 66.5% of all web pages have zero backlinks; the audit identifies what is realistic to close in 6 to 12 months.

  5. Competitor analysis (4 points)

    The top 3 organic competitors are identified and their authority scores benchmarked. The Full audit adds a top-pages comparison and a content depth analysis, quantifying the gap.

  6. Local SEO (5 points)

    Google Business Profile optimisation, NAP consistency across the web, and review score and recency, plus local citations and local schema in the Full audit. This is the section the SaaS and e-commerce audits drop; if you serve a geographic market, it is the reason to choose this audit over those.

  7. UX and conversion signals (4 points)

    Clear CTAs on key pages and navigation and site structure, plus bounce rate and session duration and intrusive pop-ups and interstitials in the Full audit. SEO traffic only matters if it converts; this section reviews where the conversion gap is.

  8. AI search and answer engine optimisation (6 points)

    Google AI Overview presence, answer engine visibility across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini, and AI crawler access and llms.txt. The Full audit adds answer-ready content structure, citation-worthy signals and E-E-A-T, and AI-focused structured data.

Tier split

The Basic audit covers 27 of the 52 points, across 7 of the 8 sections, and is right for smaller sites and first-time audits. The Full audit covers all 8 sections (52 points), adding the backlink profile and going deeper on keywords, competitors, local signals, and AI search. It is the right call when competitive positioning and authority matter.

04 / What comes back

Patterns that repeat across website audits

Anonymised examples of what the report surfaces.

  • 01

    Wrong page targeting the head term

    A homepage targeting a high-volume informational keyword that does not convert, while the actual money pages are buried three clicks deep in the navigation.

  • 02

    Duplicate title tags

    Title tags duplicated across 40+ pages, suppressing each page's ability to rank.

  • 03

    Internal linking on the wrong pages

    Internal linking concentrated on the homepage and the contact page; the actual service pages receive almost no internal link equity.

  • 04

    Core Web Vitals failing on mobile

    Core Web Vitals failing on mobile, with mobile being 60%+ of the site's traffic.

  • 05

    Blog zombies

    A blog with 80 posts where 75 receive zero organic traffic; the remaining 5 carry the whole site.

  • 06

    Competing CTAs above the fold

    A contact page with three competing CTAs above the fold, each pointing somewhere different.

The report is specific to the site. No filler.

06 / Process

How an audit takes shape, in four steps

Same shape for every audit. The detail differs by vertical; the deliverable does not.

  1. Intake

    A short brief: the site, the stage, the question you want answered. WhatsApp or email; no calls required.

  2. Crawl and tooling

    The site is crawled and analysed across the standard SaaS toolset (Google Search Console, PageSpeed, SEOptimer, structured-data validators).

  3. Manual Research

    We research your business, industry, we read your website, so we are sure we have a clear picture.

  4. Analysis

    Once the data is gathered, it's time to analyze it. The analysis is done manually, no AI.

  5. Report

    A written PDF report with scored findings, prioritised fixes, and an action plan. Delivered to your inbox.

08 / FAQ

SEO audit FAQ

The questions that come up most often before a website audit. The full FAQ has more.

What is an SEO audit and what does it include?

An SEO audit is a structured diagnosis of why your site isn't ranking, converting, or growing the way it should. The Illucrum audit covers 52 points across eight sections: technical SEO, on-page SEO, keyword opportunities, backlink profile, competitor analysis, local SEO, UX and conversion signals, and AI search readiness. The output is a prioritised, scored action plan, not a generic checklist.

How long does an SEO audit take?

Three to five business days for the Basic audit, five to eight for the Full audit. The Full version takes longer because the backlink and competitor gap analysis involves data-gathering across external tools.

Can I do my own SEO audit?

For a small site with low competition, yes, especially on the on-page side. For competitive niches or anything beyond the basics, the bottleneck is rarely knowledge; it is interpretation and prioritisation. The full answer lives at SEO audit vs DIY.

Will an SEO tool do the same job?

Tools surface symptoms; an audit interprets them. Also the tool treats each website the same. We adapt to your website type, but also your industry, to make sure we provide valuable information to you. The full comparison lives at SEO audit vs SEO tool.

What happens after the report?

You implement the findings yourself, you can also hire us to do it for you. There is no obligation to continue. The decision logic lives at SEO audit vs SEO retainer.

Is the audit confidential?

Yes. No client work is published without explicit permission, and no findings are shared externally.
