SaaS SEO audit vs standard website SEO audit: which one fits?

Both audits review your SEO foundations, both run to 52 checks, and both cost the same. What differs is which 52 checks you get. Whether the SaaS framework is the right one for your site depends on a few specific signals, laid out below.

Contact us

01 / Quick answer

Do I need a SaaS-specific SEO audit?

The featured-snippet answer, in plain language.

If your site has pricing, feature, integration, or comparison pages doing conversion work, yes; the SaaS-specific audit reviews those page types against funnel-stage benchmarks and against the comparison and alternatives content that drives most SaaS organic conversions. If your site is closer to a brochure marketing site for a SaaS product, the standard audit is the better use of the same 52 checks, because the SaaS-specific sections would have little to review.

03 / Choose SaaS

When the SaaS audit is the right framework

  • 01

    Your site has dedicated pricing, feature, or integration pages, and you suspect they are underperforming.

  • 02

    Your buyers research over weeks or months across multiple intent stages, and you want the audit to map your content surface against that funnel.

  • 03

    Trial signup conversion is the metric you care about, not just organic traffic.

The full breakdown is on the SaaS SEO audit page.

04 / Choose Website

When the standard website audit is enough

  • 01

    Your SaaS site is closer to a brochure: home, about, one features page, one pricing page, and a contact form. The funnel-stage architecture does not yet exist to be reviewed.

  • 02

    You are pre-launch or very early-stage, and you want a baseline before committing to vertical-specific scope.

  • 03

    You have a local or regional footprint that matters to the business. The website audit is the only one with a local SEO section (Google Business Profile, NAP consistency, citations, reviews, local schema); the SaaS audit drops it entirely.

05 / Honest framing

So which one should you actually pick?

The two audits cost the same and both run to 52 checks, so this is not a budget decision. It is a decision about which 52 checks are worth spending on your site. Four of the SaaS audit's eleven sections (trial and demo pages, feature and integration pages, programmatic SEO potential, content gap and blog strategy) have no counterpart in the website audit, and the website audit's local SEO section has no counterpart in the SaaS one. Pick the framework whose sections have something real to review.

For a SaaS company with a 5+ page marketing site, an active blog cluster, and at least one comparison page, the SaaS audit is almost always the better choice; those sections typically surface findings worth many times the audit itself (a single fixed pricing page or a single new comparison page can be worth tens of thousands in pipeline).

For an early-stage SaaS site with three pages and no content, the website audit is the better call; the foundations need to be in place before the SaaS-specific sections have anything to review, and four of the eleven would come back close to empty.

If you genuinely cannot tell which camp you are in, the message-and-scope approach is the safest; describe the site, and we will recommend honestly.

06 / FAQ

Common questions when deciding

Can I run both audits on the same website?

Although possible, we don't really recommend that. We are happy to adapt to our customers, you can always tell us what exactly do you need. We can always customize the audit so it fits your needs exactly.

Will the standard audit notice my SaaS-specific issues at all?

Some of them, on a foundations level (title tags, page speed, schema basics). The funnel-stage review, the trial and demo page audit, the integration and use case page architecture, and the programmatic SEO assessment are SaaS-specific work that the standard audit does not include.

Yes. Both audits are delivered in 5 to 8 business days. The SaaS-specific sections replace generic ones rather than being added on top, so the workload is comparable.
