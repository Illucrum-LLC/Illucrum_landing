Four steps, one of them on a server

Only one step runs on a server. Here's what happens at each.

  1. You enter URLs

    Nothing is sent anywhere while you type. The list is cleaned, deduplicated and capped at 25 in your browser.

  2. A relay fetches the HTML

    Browsers refuse to fetch pages from other domains. A small Cloudflare Worker on this site's domain requests each page and passes the HTML back. It stores nothing and logs nothing.

  3. Your browser does the work

    Parsing, counting, classifying, all of it happens on your machine.

  4. You export what you need

    Each table downloads as its own CSV, generated in the browser. They share a url column, so they join cleanly in a spreadsheet.

Seven tables, each one exportable

Each table covers one part of the page and downloads as its own CSV. There's no overall score, just the data.

  • Pages

    One row per URL: status, title and its length, meta description, canonical, robots directive, language, viewport, heading counts, word counts, link and image counts, every Open Graph and Twitter tag, and the structured data types found. 51 columns in the export.

  • Headings

    Every h1 through h6 in document order, with its level, position and character length. A broken outline stands out here: four h1s, or an h3 with no h2 above it.

  • Words

    The 20 most repeated words on each page, with counts and share of total. A quick way to check that a page is about what you think it's about.

  • Links

    Every anchor on the page, split into internal and external, with anchor text, rel value and whether it is nofollowed. Empty anchor text and bare “click here” links show up here.

  • Images

    Every image with its source, alt text, and whether the alt attribute is missing entirely or deliberately empty. Those are different problems and get different columns.

  • Schema

    JSON-LD, microdata and RDFa types, one row per block, with the raw JSON in the export. A block that is present but malformed is flagged rather than skipped; broken structured data is a finding.

  • Errors

    Every failure: timeouts, 404s, redirects that led nowhere, pages that were too large, JSON-LD that would not parse. Nothing is dropped silently.

What this tool does not do

Worth knowing before you rely on the results.

  • It does not run JavaScript

    Static HTML only, exactly as it arrives from the server. If your site builds its content in the browser, the tables will come back mostly empty. That's useful to know, but it isn't a verdict on your site.

  • It does not discover pages

    It fetches the URLs you type and nothing else. No sitemap parsing, no following links, no recursion. Orphan pages stay invisible.

  • It does not score anything

    No score out of 100, no grade and no traffic lights. You get the data as it is.

  • It does not check rankings, backlinks or search volume

    That data lives behind third-party APIs this tool has no connection to. Everything here comes from the page itself.

  • Word counts are approximate

    Navigation, headers and footers are stripped where they are marked up as such. On sites that do not use those elements, shared boilerplate will inflate the count.

  • Title length is characters, not pixels

    Search engines truncate titles on rendered width, not character count. The number here is a useful proxy and nothing more.

What happens to the URLs you enter

The URL goes to the relay, and nowhere else

To fetch a page, its address has to reach a server. Our relay, on Cloudflare's network, receives it, makes one request and returns the HTML. It writes nothing to a database, a log file or an analytics tool.

The page content never leaves your browser

The HTML comes back to your machine and is parsed there. Titles, headings, word counts, links, images and structured data are all derived locally. None of it is sent anywhere.

No account, and the tool stores nothing

The checker needs no sign-up and stores nothing in your browser. Close the tab and the results are gone, so download the CSVs first if you want to keep them. Like the rest of the site, this page runs Google Analytics only if you accept it in the cookie banner, and analytics never sees the addresses you enter.

The CSVs are made on your machine

Each export is assembled in the browser and handed straight to your downloads folder. No file is generated or stored on a server.

What other services can see

The site's typeface comes from CDNFonts and the review badge from Clutch, so those services see your IP address, as with any content loaded from another server. The checker works the same with them blocked.

Common questions

Why does it need a server at all?

Browsers don't let a page on one domain read a page on another. It's called the same-origin policy, and a website can't switch it off from its own side, so a small relay does the fetching.

Can I use it on a site I don't own?

Yes. It reads exactly what any visitor's browser would read, one page at a time, with a pause between requests. It's meant for a handful of pages, not a whole site.

Why only 25 URLs?

Because it runs in a browser tab. Beyond that, memory gets tight, the tables slow down, and it stops being polite to the server on the other end. For hundreds of pages, a desktop crawler is the better fit.

Does it work on a site built with React, Vue or Next.js?

Only if that site sends finished HTML. Server-rendered and statically generated pages read normally. A single-page app that draws everything after load will come back nearly empty here, and that emptiness is itself worth seeing, because a crawler that does not render will see something close to it.

Why is the word count different from another tool?

Every tool draws the line between content and boilerplate somewhere different, and none of them are wrong. This one strips nav, header, footer, script and style, and prefers main when a page has one. Compare a page against itself over time rather than against another tool's number.

Why is it free?

It's the same data collection I use at the start of an audit, and it's useful on its own. There's no account, no upgrade and no email sign-up.

Does this replace an SEO audit?

No. The tool shows what's on your pages. An audit works out which of it matters, why, and what to fix first. If the tables are all you need, that's fine.
