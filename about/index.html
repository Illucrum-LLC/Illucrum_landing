02 / Method

How a GEO audit actually takes shape

Our methodology is our essence; you can't know us without knowing how we work.

  1. We build the prompt set around your buyers

    A generic prompt list produces a generic audit. We research your category, your competitors, and the way your buyers phrase the problem, then build the queries from that.

  2. We ask the engines, repeatedly

    Every prompt runs across five assistants and both Google AI surfaces, logged out, two to three times each, because a single answer is noise. What matters is the pattern of who keeps getting named and which sources keep getting cited.

  3. We trace every gap back to a cause

    Absence from an answer is a symptom. A retrieval crawler blocked at the CDN, a page that only renders in JavaScript, a section that makes no sense read in isolation, an unverifiable author, a category where every cited source is one you have no presence on: those are causes. The report deals in causes, because only causes are fixable.

  4. Prioritizing

    Once we have a clear picture, we prioritise the issues to be fixed and the improvements worth building, to make sure your business gets the most out of the work.

04 / Commitments

What we commit to

The three things below are the operating principles behind every GEO audit. They're worth stating explicitly, for transparency.

  • 01

    Evidence, not assertion

    Every claim in a GEO report is backed by a recorded answer: the prompt, the engine, the date, and what came back. You can re-run any of it yourself. Nobody should have to take an auditor's word for what ChatGPT said last Tuesday.

  • 02

    Transparent pricing

    The audit price is on the pricing page. No discovery calls to see a number. We don't charge each client based on how much they look like they can pay. No "starting from" with hidden upsells. One price, published.

  • 03

    Implementation is your call

    The audit is a standalone deliverable. We don't push you into a retainer, and we don't think retainers make sense for most SMEs. If you want help implementing, we can scope that separately. If you want to take the report and execute it yourself, the report is written for exactly that.

05 / Approach

Why not a retainer

The default agency engagement is a monthly retainer first, an audit somewhere along the way, action items discovered as you go. That model exists because it is profitable for the agency, not because it is good for the client.

GEO is young enough that the retainer pitch is especially easy to make and especially hard to verify. Nobody can promise you a position in an AI answer, because there are no positions, and the answer is regenerated every time. A monthly fee against an outcome that cannot be guaranteed is a bad trade for a startup or an SME.

A single audit is enough to tell you whether the engines can reach you, whether your content is shaped so it can be quoted, whether your entity is clear, and where your competitors are getting their citations from. That list does not change week to week. One audit a year, maybe two if you rebuild or rebrand, is the sensible cadence.

If you are an SME or a startup, there is no point burning money on retainers. A one time audit is the way to go. The difference in price is large; the difference in results is not.
