GEO Audit vs SEO Audit: What's the Difference?

A GEO audit checks whether AI systems such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot and Google's AI Overviews can reach, read, trust and cite a business. An SEO audit checks whether a site can rank in search results and turn visits into customers. The two share foundations, since an AI system can't cite a page it can't reach, but they measure different things and often reach different conclusions. This article explains what each audit covers, where they overlap and which one to start with.

In short: An SEO audit asks whether a site can rank and convert, and covers technical health, content, keywords, links, competitors and conversion. A GEO audit asks whether AI assistants can retrieve and cite the site, and covers AI crawler access, passage structure, entity and off-site signals, and direct testing of what assistants say. Most businesses should fix SEO foundations first; GEO fits once those are in place.

What is GEO?

GEO stands for generative engine optimization: making a site retrievable, quotable and accurately described in AI-generated answers. The term comes from a 2023 research paper by researchers at Princeton and other institutions, which tested how content changes affect visibility in generative search engines. Related terms include AEO (answer engine optimization) and AI search optimization.

GEO matters because more questions are answered before anyone clicks. In a Pew Research Center study of 68,879 searches, 18% of Google searches in March 2025 produced an AI summary, and users clicked a regular result in 8% of visits with a summary against 15% without one. In BrightLocal's 2026 consumer survey, 45% of consumers said they use ChatGPT and other AI tools for local recommendations.

What is a GEO audit?

A GEO audit is a point-by-point review of what stands between a business and AI visibility. It checks whether AI crawlers can reach the site, whether pages are structured into passages an AI system can quote, whether the business is defined consistently across the web, and what assistants actually say when buyers ask.

The Illucrum GEO Audit covers 51 points in 7 sections: AI crawler and retrieval access (9), classic SEO foundations (8), passage and chunk structure (10), entity and authority (8), structured data (5), prompt visibility testing (6), and measurement (5).

What is an SEO audit?

An SEO audit is a point-by-point review of what decides whether a site is found and chosen in search results. It checks technical health, on-page SEO, keywords, backlinks, competitors, trust and conversion, and ends with a prioritized list of fixes.

Illucrum's site-type SEO audits, the Website, SaaS, E-commerce and Local Business audits, each cover 54 points. Each includes a short AI search section of four to six points, which checks AI Overviews, assistant visibility and AI crawler access at a basic level.

How does a GEO audit differ from an SEO audit?

A GEO audit and an SEO audit differ in what they treat as success. An SEO audit measures rankings and clicks; a GEO audit measures citations, recommendations and the accuracy of what AI systems say. The overlap between the two is partial: an Ahrefs study of 15,000 queries found only about 8% of URLs cited by ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot ranked in Google's top 10 for the same query.

What does a GEO audit check that an SEO audit doesn't?

A GEO audit checks four things an SEO audit covers only briefly or not at all:

  • AI crawler classes. AI companies run separate crawlers for building a search index, fetching a page a user asked about, and collecting training data. Many sites that "block AI bots" block the wrong class and remove themselves from AI answers by accident.
  • Passage structure. AI systems split pages into passages and cite the best ones. A GEO audit checks whether each section answers a question early, makes sense on its own and names its subject, rather than relying on "it" and "this".
  • Off-site presence. Assistants build answers from sources they trust: review platforms, communities, publications. A GEO audit maps which sources assistants cite for your category and whether you appear on them.
  • Prompt testing and measurement. A GEO audit runs a fixed set of buyer prompts on several assistants and records who is recommended and cited, then checks whether you can measure AI visibility afterwards with Search Console's generative AI report, Bing's AI Performance report and GA4's AI Assistant channel.

Which audit should you start with?

For most small and medium-sized businesses, start with an SEO audit. AI citations mostly go to sites that are already indexed, reasonably authoritative and mentioned on trusted sources, and structure and schema work produce little citation lift for a site without that base. An SEO audit also includes a short AI section, so the most urgent AI problems, such as a crawler block, still surface.

A GEO audit is the better starting point when:

  1. The site already ranks well in Google but rarely appears in AI answers.
  2. AI assistants describe the business inaccurately, or not at all.
  3. Buyers in your market ask AI assistants for recommendations before they search.
  4. You want a fixed prompt baseline you can rerun to track AI visibility over time.

If you only need to know whether AI crawlers can technically reach the site, the Technical SEO Audit covers that in three points, without the prompt testing.

Frequently asked questions

Is GEO replacing SEO?

No. GEO depends on SEO: an AI system can't cite a page it can't find, and pages that aren't indexed or load slowly are weak candidates for any retrieval system. GEO adds checks on top of SEO for how AI systems choose, quote and describe sources. Most businesses need the SEO foundations first and GEO work second.

Does a regular SEO audit cover AI search?

Illucrum's site-type SEO audits include four to six points on AI search: AI Overview presence, assistant visibility or recommendations, answer-ready content, entity markup and AI crawler access. That catches the most common and most urgent problems. The GEO audit covers the subject in 51 points, including passage structure, off-site signals, multi-platform prompt testing and measurement.

Can you guarantee AI assistants will recommend a business?

No one can guarantee that AI assistants will recommend or cite a business, because answers vary between runs and change over time. A GEO audit finds what stands between the business and AI visibility, ranks the fixes and records a dated baseline, but the answers themselves depend on the assistants and on the rest of the web.

Can I run a GEO audit myself?

Yes. The GEO checklist lists all 51 checks with the free tools to run them, including the prompt set and platforms to test. A first pass takes six to ten hours, most of it spent running prompts two or three times on several assistants.

An SEO audit tells you whether a site can rank and convert; a GEO audit tells you whether AI systems can reach it, quote it and describe it accurately.
