01 / Quick answer

What is a technical SEO audit?

The featured-snippet answer, in plain language.

A technical SEO audit is a focused diagnosis of the engineering and infrastructure factors that decide whether search engines can crawl, render, index, and rank a website. The Illucrum technical audit runs 36 checks across nine areas: crawlability and indexation, site architecture and URLs, JavaScript rendering, speed and Core Web Vitals, mobile and page experience, security and HTTPS, structured data and directives, international and parameter handling, and machine and AI access. The output is a written report with scored severity and prioritised fixes, delivered in 3 to 5 business days. It is the engineering layer only; it does not include keyword strategy, content, or competitor analysis.

02 / Who it's for

Who this is built for

The technical audit is the right fit when the brief is engineering, not strategy.

  • 01

    Large or complex sites

    Sites with thousands of URLs, where crawl budget, index bloat, and architecture quietly suppress rankings.

  • 02

    JavaScript-heavy applications

    Single-page apps and client-rendered sites, common with startups and SaaS teams, where content the user sees never makes it into the index.

  • 03

    After a migration or replatform

    When redirects, canonicals, and indexation need verifying before traffic erodes.

If you need keyword strategy, content depth, and competitor positioning as well, a full audit is the closer fit: the SaaS SEO audit, e-commerce SEO audit, or website SEO audit each include the technical layer plus the strategic sections this focused audit leaves out.

04 / Scope

The nine areas of a technical SEO audit

36 checks, grouped into nine areas. The technical audit goes deep on the engineering layer; every check is manual, scored on severity (green / amber / red), and prioritised by likely impact.

  1. Crawlability and indexation (7 checks)

    Robots.txt configuration, XML sitemap health, index coverage and excluded pages, HTTP status codes and server errors, redirect chains and loops, crawl budget and index bloat, and orphan pages. The gap between what is submitted and what is actually indexed. Most quiet ranking suppressors hide here.

  2. Site architecture and URLs (5 checks)

    URL structure and consistency, canonicalisation and duplicate URLs, internal linking and link equity flow, site depth and click distance, and pagination handling. Whether the most important pages are reachable, reinforced, and not competing with duplicates of themselves.

  3. Rendering and JavaScript (3 checks)

    JavaScript rendering and content parity, render-blocking resources, and lazy-loading of critical content. The routes that render fine for a user but return an empty shell to a crawler; a common cause of quietly de-indexed app subdomains.

  4. Speed and Core Web Vitals (5 checks)

    Page speed on mobile and desktop, Largest Contentful Paint, Interaction to Next Paint, Cumulative Layout Shift, image optimisation and next-gen formats, caching, compression and CDN, and third-party script weight.

  5. Mobile and page experience (3 checks)

    Mobile responsiveness, viewport and tap target sizing, and intrusive interstitials. Mobile is where most traffic now sits and where Google indexes from.

  6. Security and HTTPS (4 checks)

    HTTPS enforcement and SSL certificate health, mixed content, security headers (HSTS, required.csP), and exposed sensitive files and endpoints.

  7. Structured data and directives (3 checks)

    Structured data validity and rich result eligibility, meta robots directives, and social meta tags. Where markup is malformed or missing on templates that could earn enhanced listings, and where a stray directive is telling Google not to bother.

  8. International and parameter handling (3 checks)

    Hreflang implementation and reciprocity, www versus non-www and protocol consistency, and parameter and faceted URL handling.

  9. Machine and AI access (3 checks)

    AI crawler directives (which answer engines you are allowing or blocking, often unintentionally), llms.txt and machine-readable signals, and entity structured data (Organization and sameAs). The technical layer of GEO, without the query and content work the site-type audits cover.

Scope note

The technical audit is the engineering layer only. Keyword strategy, content depth, competitor positioning, and conversion UX are covered by the website audit and the vertical audits, not here.

08 / FAQ

Technical SEO audit FAQ

The questions that come up most often before a technical audit. The full FAQ has more.

What is a technical SEO audit?

A technical SEO audit is a focused diagnosis of the engineering and infrastructure factors that decide whether search engines can crawl, render, index, and rank your site. The Illucrum technical audit runs 36 checks across nine areas: crawlability and indexation, site architecture and URLs, JavaScript rendering, speed and Core Web Vitals, mobile and page experience, security and HTTPS, structured data and directives, international and parameter handling, and machine and AI access. The output is a prioritised, scored action plan, not a generic checklist.

How is it different from a full SEO audit?

A full SEO audit also covers keyword strategy, content depth, competitor positioning, and conversion UX. The technical audit narrows to the engineering layer only, and goes deeper on it: 36 dedicated technical points, against the broader but shallower technical section inside a site-type audit. If you need the strategic sections too, see the website SEO audit or a vertical audit, each of which includes the technical layer.

How long does a technical SEO audit take?

Three to five days from when you send the brief, depending on site size and complexity.

Will an SEO tool do the same job?

Tools surface symptoms; an audit interprets them and prioritises the fixes. The full comparison lives at SEO audit vs SEO tool.

Is the audit confidential?

Yes. No client work is published without explicit permission, and no findings are shared externally.
